## I. THRESHOLD STATUTORY ELIGIBILITY — MHL §9.60(c)

Kendra's Law has **nine conjunctive criteria**. All must be met. The most contested here will be:

### §9.60(c)(4) — The "Unlikely to Survive Safely" or "History of Non-Compliance" Prong

The statute requires either:
- **(A)** that the person has a history of lack of compliance with treatment that has, at least twice within the last 36 months, been a significant factor in necessitating hospitalization or receipt of services in a forensic or correctional setting; **OR**
- **(B)** that the person has, within the last 48 months, engaged in one or more acts of serious violent behavior toward self or others, or threats of, or attempts at, serious physical harm to self or others.

**The factual tension here is significant:**

- The **October 2024 and October 2025 hospitalizations** appear to satisfy the "twice within 36 months" requirement on their face — but the record needs to establish that **non-compliance was a significant factor** in each, not merely that hospitalizations occurred.
- The **April 5, 2026 §9.41 episode** is *not* a hospitalization — she was released after four hours and did not meet §9.39 inpatient criteria. Petitioner's counsel should be careful not to overstate this event's legal weight under (c)(4), though it may be relevant to other prongs.
- MHLS will likely scrutinize whether the clinic closure (which precipitated the LAI lapse) constitutes *her* non-compliance or a **systemic failure** — this is a meaningful distinction both legally and equitably.

### §9.60(c)(5) — Unlikely to Survive Safely in the Community Without Supervision

This requires that Maya is "unlikely to survive safely in the community without supervision." The April 5 episode (outside in 38°F without a coat, responding to internal stimuli) is the petitioner's strongest recent evidence here, but MHLS will note she **was not hospitalized**, which cuts both ways.

### §9.60(c)(6) — Condition Is Substantially Deteriorating
