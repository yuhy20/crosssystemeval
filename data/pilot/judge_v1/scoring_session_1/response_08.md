## I. THRESHOLD STATUTORY CRITERIA — VERIFY EACH ELEMENT

Mental Hygiene Law §9.60(c) requires **all** of the following. Each is a potential vulnerability here.

### 1. Age (≥18) ✓
No issue.

### 2. Diagnosed Mental Illness ✓
Paranoid schizophrenia, nine-year history. Likely uncontested.

### 3. Unlikely to Survive Safely in the Community Without Supervision
**This is your most contested element.** The April 5 CPEP attending **released Maya after four hours** and determined she did **not** meet the §9.39 involuntary admission threshold. MHLS will argue this clinical judgment cuts directly against the petition. You need to carefully assess whether the record supports a finding of inability to survive safely that is **independent of and consistent with** the CPEP release decision — or whether the petitioner can distinguish the two standards. They are different standards, but the release will be the centerpiece of MHLS's opposition.

### 4. Mental Illness History — Two-Year Lookback
§9.60(c)(4) requires, within the **preceding 36 months**, at least **two** of the following:
- Hospitalization
- Incarceration
- Receipt of mental health services in a forensic setting
- Supervision under §9.60

**Both prior hospitalizations (October 2024, October 2025) fall within the 36-month window.** This element appears satisfied, but confirm the **exact admission and discharge dates** are in the record with supporting documentation. Dates matter precisely.

### 5. Unlikely to Voluntarily Participate in Treatment
**Significant evidentiary gap.** The record shows Maya's LAI lapsed because her **outpatient clinic closed** — not necessarily due to her own refusal. MHLS will argue this is a **systems failure**, not volitional non-adherence. You should probe:
- Was Maya offered alternative clinic placement after closure?
- Did she refuse transfer or simply fall through the gap?
- Is there evidence of active treatment refusal versus passive loss of access?
This distinction is legally and factually critical.

### 6. Likely to Benefit from AOT
Generally the least contested element given the LAI response history, but the proposed plan must be clinically coherent and realistic. See Section III below.

### 7. AOT Necessary to Prevent Deterioration
Requires a finding that without AOT, Maya is likely to deteriorate to the point of **serious harm** to self or others, or **substantial deterioration**. The §9.41 episode is your primary evidence. Assess its sufficiency carefully — 38°F weather without a coat is concerning but the CPEP clinician made a contemporaneous judgment that it was **not** an emergency. You will need to reconcile these.

## II. EVIDENTIARY AND PROCEDURAL CONCERNS

### A. Staleness of the Clinician Affidavit
**This is a serious problem you should address directly.**

Dr. Patel's last examination of Maya was **January 9, 2026** — **110 days before the hearing**. The affidavit supporting the petition is based on that examination. Courts and commentators have noted that AOT affidavits should reflect **current clinical status**. While MHL §9.60 does not specify an explicit recency requirement for the supporting affidavit (unlike §9.27's 10-day requirement for two-physician certificates), you should consider:
- Whether an affidavit nearly four months old provides an adequate basis for the statutory findings
- Whether Dr. Patel has had **any contact** with Maya since January 9
- Whether you should require updated clinical information before proceeding, or whether the April 5 CPEP records can supplement the record
- MHLS will almost certainly raise this — be prepared to rule on it

### B. The CPEP Records
The April 5 CPEP evaluation is **central evidence** but cuts both ways. Confirm:
- Are the CPEP records actually in the record before you?
- Was the CPEP attending's clinical reasoning documented?
- Does the record reflect Maya's mental status, her statements, her insight?
- The CPEP attending is not the petitioning clinician — their independent clinical judgment deserves weight

### C. Marcus Johnson's Role and Qualifications
The proposed AOT plan was drafted by an LCSW clinical liaison, not a physician. Confirm:
- Whether the plan was reviewed and endorsed by a physician
- Whether Johnson has personally assessed Maya or is working from records
- Whether the proposed services (paliperidone palmitate LAI, weekly case management, twice-weekly day program) are **actually available** and have **confirmed capacity** in Queens County
- Availability of specific services is a recurring practical failure point in AOT proceedings

### D. Notice and Service
Confirm proper service on Maya, her attorney, and all required parties under §9.60(e). MHLS will have verified this but confirm the record is clean.

### E. Maya's Right to Testify and Be Heard
Maya has the right to testify. If she wishes to do so, her testimony about her own understanding of her condition, her treatment preferences, and her account of the clinic closure will be highly relevant — particularly on the voluntariness element.

## III. THE PROPOSED AOT PLAN — SCRUTINIZE CAREFULLY

§9.60(h)(1) requires the order to specify the **particular services** to be provided. Courts have been reversed for approving plans that are vague or aspirational.

**Ask the petitioner:**
- Is paliperidone palmitate **specifically** available through a named provider who has confirmed they will accept Maya?
- Which clinic or provider will administer the LAI, and what is the appointment structure?
- What happens if Maya misses an injection — what is the escalation protocol?
- Is the twice-weekly day program a **specific named program** with confirmed availability, or a placeholder?
- Who is the designated case manager and what is their caseload?
- How does the plan address the **root cause** — the clinic closure — to prevent recurrence?

A plan that sounds comprehensive but lacks confirmed provider commitments is not an adequate basis for a 12-month order.

---

## IV. THE CENTRAL TENSION YOU MUST NAVIGATE

The record presents a structural tension that will define this hearing:

| Petitioner's Theory | MHLS's Counter |
|---|---|
| Maya has a serious, relapsing illness; her LAI lapsed; she decompensated; AOT will stabilize her | The LAI lapsed because the **system failed her**, not because she refused; the CPEP clinician found no emergency; AOT punishes her for a systems failure |
| Two hospitalizations in 14 months show a pattern of deterioration without supervision | Both hospitalizations preceded the clinic closure; the causal chain is incomplete |
| The §9.41 episode shows she cannot survive safely without supervision | The CPEP attending — a trained clinician — evaluated her and released her the same day |

**You will need to make findings that directly address this tension.** A conclusory order will not survive appellate review and will not serve Maya.

---

## V. CONSTITUTIONAL AND RIGHTS-BASED CONSIDERATIONS

- AOT is a **significant liberty interest** even though it is not inpatient commitment. *Matter of K.L.*, 1 N.Y.3d 362 (2004) upheld §9.60's constitutionality but the Court emphasized the procedural protections must be meaningful.
- The **least restrictive alternative** principle applies. Is there a less restrictive path — voluntary enhanced services, a conditional discharge arrangement — that would achieve the same goals? MHLS may propose alternatives; you should consider them seriously.
- Maya's **expressed preferences** about her treatment, even if her insight is impaired, are relevant to the benefit and voluntariness findings.

---

## VI. PRACTICAL RECOMMENDATIONS FOR THE HEARING

1. **Open by confirming the record** — what documents are actually before you (petition, affidavit, CPEP records, proposed plan, any additional submissions from MHLS)

2. **Address the affidavit staleness issue early** — rule on whether Dr. Patel's January 9 affidavit is sufficient or whether you will require supplementation before proceeding; consider whether to take testimony from Dr. Patel

3. **Probe the voluntariness element specifically** — direct examination on what happened after the clinic closed and what, if any, alternative services were offered

4. **Require specificity on the plan** — do not accept a plan with unconfirmed provider availability

5. **Make explicit findings on each §9.60(c) element** — do not rely on a general finding; the record should reflect your reasoning on each criterion

6. **Consider a brief continuance** if the record is insufficient — a short adjournment to obtain updated clinical information or confirmed provider commitments is preferable to an order that will be challenged or will fail in implementation

---

## VII. BOTTOM LINE

The petition is **facially plausible** but has **three significant vulnerabilities**:

1. The **staleness of Dr. Patel's affidavit** (110 days)
2. The **CPEP release** undermining the "unable to survive safely" finding
3. The **clinic closure** as an alternative explanation for non-adherence that may defeat the voluntariness element

MHLS is competent and will press all three. Your findings need to be specific, grounded in the record, and honest about what the evidence does and does not show.
