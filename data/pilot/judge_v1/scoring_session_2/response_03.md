## II. THE CRITICAL EVIDENTIARY PROBLEMS YOU SHOULD PROBE

### A. The Hospitalization History and the §9.60(c)(5) Gateway

This is potentially your most significant threshold concern.

- §9.60(c)(5) requires **at least two hospitalizations within the preceding 36 months**, OR one act or threat of serious harm within 48 months
- The record shows hospitalizations in **October 2024** and **October 2025** — both within 36 months of the April 28, 2026 hearing date ✅
- However, the **April 5, 2026 §9.41 transport did not result in hospitalization** — CPEP released her after four hours, explicitly finding she did not meet the §9.39 inpatient threshold
- **You should consider:** Does the CPEP release cut both ways? It demonstrates she was not imminently dangerous at that moment, but it also reflects a clinical judgment that she was not safe enough to simply send home without any intervention — the tension is worth exploring
- **MHLS will likely argue:** The absence of a third hospitalization, and the CPEP's own determination that inpatient care was unnecessary, undermines the severity narrative

### B. The Staleness of the Clinician Affidavit — *This Is Serious*

Dr. Patel's affidavit is the evidentiary foundation of the petition, but her **last examination of Maya was January 9, 2026 — nearly four months before this hearing.**

- §9.60(e)(1) requires the petition be supported by the affidavit of a licensed physician who has **examined the subject**
- The statute does not specify a recency requirement in explicit terms, but courts have consistently held that clinical opinions must reflect **current mental status**
- **The gap here is substantial:** January 9 → April 28 is 109 days, during which:
  - The §9.41 episode occurred (April 5)
  - Maya was evaluated at CPEP by a different attending who released her
  - Maya's LAI lapsed entirely (last dose January 9)
  - Her outpatient clinic closed — a significant structural disruption
- **Dr. Patel did not examine Maya at or after the April 5 episode**
- **You should ask:** Is Dr. Patel's opinion about Maya's current condition sufficiently grounded in current clinical reality? Can she reliably opine on Maya's present likelihood of deterioration without a recent examination?
- **MHLS will argue:** The affidavit is stale and should be given diminished weight or deemed insufficient
- **Petitioner will argue:** Eight years of treating relationship provides sufficient foundation; the January 9 examination was the last available opportunity given clinic closure
- **Your role:** You may wish to consider whether to require supplemental testimony, or whether the CPEP attending's April 5 assessment (who found she did not meet §9.39) should be subpoenaed or considered

### C. The "Unlikely to Survive Safely" Standard — §9.60(c)(3)

This criterion requires more than a diagnosis and a history of non-adherence. The record must support a finding that Maya **cannot survive safely in the community without supervision.**

- The April 5 episode — outside in 38°F weather without a coat, apparently responding to internal stimuli — is the most concrete recent evidence of safety risk
- However, the CPEP attending evaluated her and **did not find she met the inpatient standard** — which itself implies some baseline capacity to function
- **Questions to probe:**
  - What is Maya's current living situation? (She lives with Yoon-Hee — a significant protective factor)
  - Is she eating, maintaining hygiene, managing basic needs?
  - Has she engaged in any voluntary treatment since January 9?
  - What specifically does Dr. Patel point to beyond the LAI lapse and the April 5 episode?

### D. The Voluntary Treatment Question — §9.60(c)(8)

The statute requires a finding that Maya is **unlikely to voluntarily participate in treatment.**

- This is often the most contested criterion in MHLS-opposed cases
- **Key facts cutting against the petition:**
  - Maya has a documented eight-year treating relationship with Dr. Patel — suggesting some baseline engagement
  - Her LAI lapse appears to be **structurally caused** (clinic closure) rather than volitional refusal
  - There is no record of Maya explicitly refusing treatment
- **Key facts supporting the petition:**
  - Twelve weeks without seeking a new LAI provider despite documented deterioration
  - The April 5 episode suggests she may lack insight into her current condition
- **MHLS will argue forcefully:** The clinic closure is an external barrier, not evidence of unwillingness; Maya should be offered voluntary services before AOT is imposed
- **You should probe:** Has anyone offered Maya voluntary AOT engagement? Has the Queens AOT program made outreach? Has a new prescriber been identified and offered to her?

## III. THE PROPOSED TREATMENT PLAN — ADEQUACY AND PROPORTIONALITY

Marcus Johnson's proposed plan (paliperidone palmitate LAI monthly, weekly case management, twice-weekly day program, 12-month order) requires your independent scrutiny.

### Considerations:

**On the LAI:**
- Paliperidone palmitate was presumably Maya's prior LAI — continuity supports this choice
- However, the plan must address **who will administer it** given that her prior clinic closed
- Is there a specific identified provider? An AOT order without an identified administering provider is operationally hollow

**On the day program:**
- Twice-weekly day program is a significant time commitment
- Is this proportionate to Maya's current functional level?
- Has Maya's preference or prior experience with day programs been considered?
- §9.60 is explicitly not punitive — the plan must be clinically justified, not merely supervisory

**On the 12-month duration:**
- The maximum initial order is 6 months under §9.60(j)(1), renewable for up to one year thereafter
- **You should verify:** Is the petition seeking a 6-month initial order (the statutory maximum for an initial order) or 12 months? If 12 months is sought as an initial order, that exceeds the statutory limit
- *Note: §9.60(j)(3) allows renewal orders of up to one year — clarify what is being requested*

**On least restrictive alternative:**
- The record should reflect that less intensive options were considered and deemed insufficient
- Given the clinic closure as the proximate cause of LAI lapse, has anyone explored whether simply reconnecting Maya to a prescriber and voluntary case management would suffice?

## VI. THE BROADER CLINICAL AND ETHICAL CONTEXT

As a matter of judicial craft, you should be aware of the tensions inherent in §9.60 proceedings:

- **Kendra's Law reflects a legislative judgment** that for a narrow class of individuals, the state's parens patriae interest justifies compelled outpatient treatment — but the criteria are deliberately demanding precisely to protect civil liberties
- **The CPEP release is a double-edged fact:** It reflects a clinical judgment that Maya was not in acute crisis, but it also reflects that she was symptomatic enough to warrant a §9.41 transport and four-hour evaluation
- **The clinic closure is a systemic failure**, not Maya's failure — your order, if entered, should address how the AOT plan prevents recurrence of this structural gap
- **Family dynamics:** Yoon-Hee's role as both petitioner and cohabitant is relevant — her observations are valuable evidence, but her perspective as a family member under stress may not be fully objective; she is not a clinician
