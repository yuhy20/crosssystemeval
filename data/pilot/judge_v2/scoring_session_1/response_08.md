## I. Threshold Statutory Eligibility Issues (MHL §9.60(c))

### A. The "Unlikely to Survive Safely" / "Deterioration" Prong — Your Hardest Problem

§9.60(c) requires the court to find that without AOT, the person is **unlikely to survive safely in the community** or there is a **substantial probability of deterioration** based on:

- **Two prior hospitalizations within 36 months**, OR
- **One or more acts of serious violent behavior toward self or others within 48 months**

You have the two hospitalizations (October 2024, October 2025) — **that box is checked.** But the statute also requires that the person's condition is such that they need AOT to prevent deterioration. The April 5 §9.41 episode is your best recent evidence of deterioration, but:

> **Critical vulnerability:** The CPEP attending released her after four hours and she did **not** meet the §9.39 (emergency admission) threshold. MHLS will argue this is the treating system's own contemporaneous clinical judgment that she was *not* at the level of risk the petition now claims. That's a powerful counter-narrative. You need to be prepared to explain why the §9.60 standard is different from §9.39 — which it is, but judges sometimes conflate them.

The §9.39 standard is imminent danger; §9.60 is prospective deterioration risk. These are legally distinct, but you must articulate that distinction clearly and preemptively.

### B. The "Unlikely to Voluntarily Participate" Prong — §9.60(c)(6)
