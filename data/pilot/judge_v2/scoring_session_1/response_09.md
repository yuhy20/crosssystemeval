# Critical Issues for AOT §9.60 Petition - Queens County

This petition presents several significant vulnerabilities. Here's what requires careful attention:

## **1. STALE CLINICAL EVIDENCE (Major Problem)**

**The core issue:** Dr. Patel's last examination was **January 9, 2026**—over 3.5 months before the hearing. Under §9.60(c), the petition must be supported by "clear and convincing evidence" of current dangerousness/grave disability.

- The April 5 §9.41 transport is *not* a hospitalization; CPEP release means she didn't meet §9.39 criteria
- One incident of being outside without a coat in cold weather, while concerning, is thin evidence of *current* grave disability
- **MHLS will argue:** The stale affidavit doesn't establish current mental state; conditions may have changed; no recent psychiatric evaluation
- **You need:** A recent examination (ideally within 2 weeks) by the affiant or another qualified clinician documenting current symptoms, functional decline, and specific dangerousness/grave disability

**Statutory requirement:** MHL §9.60(c) requires the affidavit to be based on "personal examination" and establish current criteria.

---

## **2. MEDICATION ADHERENCE vs. DANGEROUSNESS (Evidentiary Gap)**

The petition conflates two different things:

- **What you have:** Maya stopped LAI in January; she had one concerning incident in April
- **What you need for AOT:** Clear and convincing evidence that she is *currently* dangerous to herself/others OR gravely disabled *because of mental illness*

**MHLS will argue:**
- Missing one LAI dose doesn't automatically equal dangerousness
- The April 5 incident alone may not meet the "substantial likelihood of serious harm" threshold
- No evidence of violence, threats, or inability to meet basic needs (she lives with her mother who supports her)

**Critical question:** What specific behaviors/symptoms in the last 30 days demonstrate current dangerousness or grave disability? The petition must be specific.

---

## **3. CLOSURE OF OUTPATIENT CLINIC (Procedural/Equitable Issue)**

- Maya's clinic closed *before* she missed her January appointment
- This is a systems failure, not necessarily her non-compliance
- **MHLS argument:** The state/clinic system failed to maintain continuity; AOT shouldn't be the remedy for inadequate outpatient infrastructure
- **Judge Williams may ask:** Why wasn't she re-linked to care immediately after clinic closure? What efforts were made?

This weakens the "less restrictive alternative exhausted" showing.

## **5. LESS RESTRICTIVE ALTERNATIVES (LRA) - UNDERDEVELOPED**

- Outpatient treatment is appropriate
