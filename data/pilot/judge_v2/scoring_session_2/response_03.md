# Critical Legal and Procedural Issues for Your §9.60 Hearing

Judge Williams, this petition presents several significant vulnerabilities that require careful attention:

## 1. **Stale Clinical Evidence (Most Critical)**

Dr. Patel's last examination was **January 9, 2026**—nearly **4 months before this hearing**. This is problematic:

- The §9.60 standard requires "clear and convincing evidence" of current dangerousness/grave disability
- A single exam from 4 months ago, with no intervening clinical contact, is thin evidentiary support
- The April 5 §9.41 transport and CPEP release (which found her *not* meeting §9.39 criteria) occurred *after* Dr. Patel's last contact
- MHLS will argue the clinical picture has changed and the affidavit is stale

**You should require:**
- Live testimony from Dr. Patel (not just the affidavit) with cross-examination
- Explanation for why she hasn't examined Maya since January
- Specific current clinical observations, not historical narrative

## 2. **The CPEP Release Problem**

The April 5 CPEP attending released Maya after 4 hours, finding she did **not** meet §9.39 criteria (imminent danger). This creates tension:

- If she wasn't imminently dangerous on April 5, what has changed in 23 days to now warrant AOT?
- The petition relies heavily on *past* hospitalizations (Oct 2024, Oct 2025) and *past* non-compliance
- MHLS will argue this shows the system worked—she was evaluated and released appropriately

**You need:**
- Testimony explaining the distinction between §9.39 (imminent danger) and §9.60 (likelihood of serious harm without treatment)
- Specific deterioration *since* April 5, not just the April 5 incident itself

## 3. **Medication Non-Compliance vs. Lack of Access**

The narrative shows:
- Last LAI dose: January 9, 2026
- Outpatient clinic closed shortly after
- No documented attempts to re-engage her in treatment for 3+ months

This is critical:

- Was non-compliance *volitional* (she refused treatment) or *structural* (clinic closed, no alternative offered)?
- If the clinic closed without arranging continuity of care, that's a system failure, not evidence of her untreatable condition
- §9.60 is meant for people who refuse treatment, not those abandoned by the system

**You should probe:**
- What efforts were made to re-engage her after the clinic closure?
- Were there alternative clinics/providers offered?
- Is the non-compliance documented or inferred?

## 4. **Insufficient Specificity on "Grave Disability"**

The petition mentions:
- She was outside in 38°F weather without a coat
- She was "talking to no one visible"

But:
- Was she unable to care for basic needs (food, shelter, hygiene)?
- Does she have housing, income, family support (her mother lives with her)?
- The mother's presence suggests *some* support system exists

**You need clear testimony on:**
- Specific instances of inability to provide for food, shelter, clothing, or medical care
- Not just bizarre behavior or poor judgment

## 5. **Procedural/Constitutional Safeguards**

Ensure the record reflects:

- **Right to counsel**: MHLS is present—good. But confirm Maya understands she can testify and cross-examine.
- **Clear and convincing standard**: This is *higher* than preponderance. Stale evidence and the CPEP release make this a heavy lift.
- **Least restrictive alternative**: Has outpatient treatment (without the coercive element) been tried *recently*? Or only the failed clinic from January?
- **Dangerousness specificity**: "Likelihood of serious harm" requires more than general psychiatric history.

## 6. **The Mother's Petition**

Yoon-Hee filed the petition. Consider:

- What is her relationship with Maya? (Living together is good, but is there conflict?)
- Is she testifying? If so, MHLS will cross-examine her motivations.
- Does she have capacity to support Maya in treatment without AOT?

## 7. **Red Flags for Reversal on Appeal**

If you grant AOT, MHLS will likely appeal on:
- Stale clinical evidence
- CPEP's contrary finding
- Lack of specificity on current dangerousness
- Inadequate exploration of less restrictive alternatives
