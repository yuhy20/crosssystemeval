Additionally, the court must determine that she has a history of non-compliance with treatment and that the proposed treatment is necessary to prevent a deterioration of her condition.

2. **Evidence and Documentation**: Ensure that all evidence supporting the petition is well-documented and presented clearly. This includes Dr. Patel's affidavit, which should detail Maya's diagnosis, treatment history, and the rationale for the proposed AOT plan. The affidavit should also address her recent behavior and the risks associated with her non-compliance.

3. **Maya's Rights and Representation**: Be aware that Maya has the right to contest the petition and to be represented by counsel from the Mental Hygiene Legal Service (MHLS). It is crucial to respect her rights throughout the process and to ensure that her voice is heard in the proceedings.

4. **Opposition from Counsel**: Prepare for the arguments that Maya's counsel may present in opposition to the petition. They may argue that she does not meet the criteria for AOT, that she is capable of making her own treatment decisions, or that the proposed treatment is not appropriate or necessary.

5. **Focus on Recent Behavior**: Highlight the significance of Maya's recent behavior, including the incident that led to the §9.41 transport and her lack of compliance with her long-acting injectable (LAI) medication. This behavior may be critical in demonstrating the need for AOT.

6. **Treatment Plan Feasibility**: Be prepared to discuss the feasibility and appropriateness of the proposed treatment plan, including the monthly LAI injections, weekly case management, and participation in a day program. Address any potential concerns about her ability to adhere to this plan.

7. **Judicial Considerations**: Understand Judge Williams' approach to mental health cases and any specific preferences she may have regarding the presentation of evidence or arguments. Tailoring your approach to her style may be beneficial.

8. **Potential Outcomes**: Be prepared for various outcomes, including the possibility that the court may grant the petition, deny it, or request additional information or a follow-up hearing. Consider what steps will be taken in each scenario.

9. **Community Resources**: Familiarize yourself with available community resources and support systems that could be beneficial for Maya, whether or not the AOT petition is granted. This may help in demonstrating a commitment to her well-being.

10. **Emotional Considerations**: Recognize the emotional aspects of the case, both for Maya and her family. The hearing can be a stressful experience, and being sensitive to these dynamics can help facilitate a more constructive dialogue.

By carefully considering these factors, you can better prepare for the hearing and advocate effectively for the AOT petition while respecting Maya's rights and needs.
