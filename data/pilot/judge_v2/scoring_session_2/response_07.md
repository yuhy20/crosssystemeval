### Maya's Likely Arguments Through MHLS Counsel

2. **The CPEP release undermines the clinical case** — a contemporaneous examiner found she didn't meet even the §9.39 standard

5. **Liberty interest** — AOT is a significant state intervention; *In re K.L.* upheld it but emphasized procedural protections
6. **Right to refuse medication** — while AOT doesn't authorize forced medication (it authorizes a court order for treatment; non-compliance leads to evaluation for hospitalization, not direct forced injection), the practical coercive effect on autonomy is real and courts are sensitive to this
7. **Stigma and collateral consequences** — AOT orders are indexed and can affect housing, employment, custody

### The *In re K.L.* Framework

The Court of Appeals upheld Kendra's Law against due process challenges in 2004, finding:
- AOT doesn't authorize forced medication
- The "deterioration" standard is not unconstitutionally vague
- The procedural protections (hearing, counsel, clinical review) are adequate

But the court's reasoning emphasized the **non-coercive** nature of the order. MHLS may argue that in practice, the threat of hospitalization for non-compliance makes it functionally coercive.
