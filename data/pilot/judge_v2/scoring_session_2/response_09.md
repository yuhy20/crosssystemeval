## I. THRESHOLD LEGAL SUFFICIENCY ISSUES

### A. The Staleness Problem — Dr. Patel's Affidavit

This is your most significant vulnerability on the record.

**MHL §9.60(c)(2)** requires the supporting clinician affidavit to be based on an examination. Dr. Patel last examined Maya on **January 9, 2026** — **109 days before the hearing**. Courts and MHLS routinely challenge affidavits where the examination is not reasonably contemporaneous with the petition or hearing.

**What to watch for:**
- MHLS will almost certainly move to strike or discount the Patel affidavit on staleness grounds
- The CPEP attending who saw Maya on April 5 *did not* find her to meet §9.39 criteria — that clinician's assessment, while not dispositive for AOT, cuts against the clinical picture Dr. Patel may be painting
- You should consider whether the record contains any examination closer in time to the hearing, and if not, whether the affidavit alone is legally sufficient to satisfy the statutory evidentiary requirement
- **Practical question:** Was Dr. Patel available to testify live tomorrow? Live testimony would partially cure the staleness concern

**The tension you must resolve:** The CPEP release after four hours is a double-edged fact. It shows Maya was not acutely dangerous enough for inpatient commitment, which MHLS will use to argue she doesn't meet AOT criteria — but the AOT standard is explicitly *lower* than §9.39, which is precisely the legislative purpose of Kendra's Law.

### B. Statutory Criteria — Each Element Requires Independent Support

Under **MHL §9.60(c)**, you must find by **clear and convincing evidence** that Maya meets *all* of the following. Scrutinize each:

| Criterion | Statutory Requirement | Record Support | Vulnerability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age | 18+ | ✓ (32) | None |

**The "least restrictive alternative" element deserves particular attention.** This is constitutionally grounded and MHLS will press it. The record should affirmatively show why voluntary outpatient treatment is insufficient — not merely that Maya has been noncompliant, but *why* AOT rather than continued voluntary engagement would make a difference.

### C. The Clinic Closure — A Structural Complication

Maya's LAI lapsed because **her outpatient clinic closed**, not solely due to volitional noncompliance. This is legally and ethically significant.

**Why this matters:**
- MHL §9.60(c)(4) focuses on the subject's *unlikely compliance* — if the lapse was system-caused rather than patient-caused, the predictive inference about future noncompliance is weakened
- MHLS will argue this distinguishes Maya from the paradigmatic Kendra's Law subject
- You should probe whether the proposed AOT plan has identified a *stable, funded provider* for the paliperidone palmitate LAI — if the infrastructure failed her once, ordering AOT without addressing that is problematic

**Questions you may want answered at hearing:**

- Was Maya offered alternative providers?
- Did she seek care independently after the closure?
- Is the proposed LAI provider confirmed and funded?

## IV. CONSTITUTIONAL AND POLICY OVERLAY

### A. Liberty Interest

AOT is a significant deprivation of liberty even without physical confinement. *Addington v. Texas* and its progeny require that civil commitment-adjacent proceedings satisfy due process. While AOT has been upheld (*In re K.L.*, 1 N.Y.3d 362 (2004)), the clear and convincing standard must be genuinely applied, not treated as a rubber stamp.

### B. The *In re K.L.* Framework

The Court of Appeals upheld Kendra's Law in *K.L.* on the ground that AOT is non-punitive and the least restrictive alternative to hospitalization. Your findings should track this framework explicitly if you grant the petition — particularly the least restrictive alternative finding.

### C. Racial and Structural Equity Awareness

This is not a legal element, but it is worth your awareness as a matter of judicial craft:
- AOT petitions are filed disproportionately against people of color
- Maya Chen is a Chinese-American woman in Flushing; her mother is the petitioner, which is a different family dynamic than many AOT cases
- The clinic closure that precipitated her LAI lapse may reflect systemic underfunding of mental health infrastructure in immigrant communities
- None of this changes the legal analysis, but it should inform how you weigh the "least restrictive alternative" finding and whether the proposed plan is genuinely designed to serve Maya's interests
