# Authorship & credit policy

This is a template. Pick one option in each section, fill in the bracketed details, and copy the result into the lawyer and TASC briefs verbatim. Same policy goes to both unless there's a strong reason to differ.

---

## What kind of engagement is this?

Pick one:

- [ ] **Acknowledgement-only.** "Your contribution will be acknowledged by name in the paper's Acknowledgements section. You will not be a coauthor." — Use when the helper is contributing < 2 hours of their time and the work is consultative rather than substantive.

- [ ] **Coauthorship.** "If your contribution becomes substantive — e.g., your critique meaningfully revises the rubric, or your scoring becomes part of the inter-rater agreement statistic in the paper — I will offer coauthorship in the order: [YOU], [HELPER], [ANY OTHERS]." — Use when the helper's contribution may genuinely shape methodology.

- [ ] **Paid consultation.** "I will pay you $[RATE]/hr, capped at [HOURS]. No coauthorship; you may be acknowledged or not at your preference." — Use when the helper prefers an arms-length professional engagement.

## Conflict-of-interest disclosure

> "If you have any current or prior involvement with a §9.60 / Kendra's Law case (counsel, expert witness, party, family), let me know now. It probably doesn't disqualify you, but it should be disclosed in the paper's competing-interests section."

(Recommended for the lawyer; optional for the TASC employee.)

## Data and confidentiality

> "The LLM responses you'll see are generated from a fictional fact pattern (Maya Chen, etc.) — no real people. The fact pattern is published in our public GitHub repo. Your scores and any notes you provide will be saved in that same public repo unless you ask otherwise. If you'd like your name kept off the public commit history and only included in the paper acknowledgements, say so and I'll keep your contribution as a private file."

## What you'll get back from me

> "After scoring is done, I'll send you: (1) a 1-page summary of how your scores compared to mine and what changed in the rubric as a result; (2) a draft of the methods section that incorporates your contribution, before submission; (3) a final copy of the paper when it's done."

## Time commitment

> "I'm asking for [N] hours of your time. If at any point during the work you feel this is taking longer than that or isn't fitting your expertise, please tell me — I'd rather stop early than have you finish reluctantly."
