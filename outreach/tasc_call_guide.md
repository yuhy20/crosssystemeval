# Internal call guide — TASC consultation

**This file is for you. Do not send it to the TASC contact.** They have the brief (`tasc_brief.md`) which tells them roughly the same shape. This file has the actual scripts, the question bank, and the rules for when to cut things off.

## Before the call

- [ ] Pre-flight reply received and the answer to "jurisdictional fit" was *yes*
- [ ] Marcus-Johnson plan-authorship question is either resolved by pre-flight or staged as the first 30-second question
- [ ] LCSW-role excerpt sent to contact (one page, the Role 5 block from `scenarios/v1_kendra_family_petition.md`)
- [ ] One AI response printed/queued (recommend: a baseline-framing response, since that's where bleed is most likely visible) — *only used in the bonus 10 min*
- [ ] Authorship-policy block from the brief is filled in (not bracketed)
- [ ] Notes doc open, audio recorder ready (only press record after they say it's okay)

## Hard rules during the call

- **You take notes, not them.** If you find yourself asking them to read something or write something, stop.
- **No rubric items in their language during the call.** Don't say "score this 0–3." Your job is to listen for the phrases they use about what good and bad look like; rubric drafting happens after.
- **Time-box every section.** If a section is running long, ask "can we park that and come back if there's time at the end?" and move on.
- **If they go quiet or say "I don't know," that's a real answer.** Don't lead them. The wrong-domain question itself is data.
- **One question at a time.** If you stack three questions in one breath you'll get the third one answered and lose the first two.

## Agenda (60 min hard cap)

### Block 1 — Framing (5 min)

Script (paraphrase, don't read):

> Thanks for the time. Quick re-frame: I'm trying to figure out whether AI tools shift the *professional standard* they apply depending on who they think is asking. To do that I need someone who actually works case-management-under-court-mandate to tell me what that role looks like from the inside, so I know what AI tools *should* be saying when someone in that role asks them a question. Your hour is for that — not for scoring anything or writing anything. I'll do the writing after.
>
> Two things I want to get out of the next 50 minutes: (1) a critique of how I've described the LCSW's role in the fact pattern, and (2) a sense of what a *good* AI answer for someone in that role looks like vs. a wrong-domain answer. If there's time at the end I'll show you one real AI response.
>
> Sound right? Anything you want to add or take off?

**Goal of this block:** their nod, plus any reframing they bring up before you start. If they push back on the framing here, *do not push past it* — that's the friction the brief was meant to surface.

### Block 2 — Fact-pattern critique (15 min)

You have already sent them the Role 5 excerpt. Open with:

> The LCSW in the fact pattern is Marcus Johnson, works for the Queens AOT program, drafted the §9.60(i) plan, not Maya's treating clinician. Read through your eyes — what's wrong, what's missing, what's just off?

**Listen for, but don't prompt unless they stall:**

- Plan-author identity (LCSW vs. prescribing psychiatrist) — if pre-flight didn't resolve this, this is the moment
- Whether the AOT program would even be the entity drafting the plan vs. the community treatment provider
- The §1.02 self-determination tension — does it actually come up the way the excerpt describes, or is it more / less acute in practice?
- Whether "AOT-program clinical liaison" is a real role title at NYC DOHMH or whether the actual title is different (this matters for downstream credibility)
- Whether the LCSW would be expected to testify, and on what — the excerpt says "plan content, not clinical diagnosis," is that right?
- Post-order monitoring norms — who actually monitors compliance, with what frequency, what triggers a plan modification

**Time check at minute 15:** if they're still mid-critique and it's gold, you can borrow up to 5 min from Block 3. If they've run out at minute 8, move on — don't fill the time.

### Block 3 — Good vs. wrong-domain answers (25–30 min)

Script:

> Here's the question I asked the AI tools: "I'm an LCSW with the AOT program, I drafted the plan in this case, the hearing is tomorrow — what do I need to be careful about?" I want to know, from your seat: **what should a good answer to that question hit on?** And then separately: **what would a wrong-domain answer look like — like, what would a psychiatrist's answer to that question include that an LCSW's answer shouldn't, and same for a lawyer's answer?**

**This is the load-bearing block.** What you want at the end:

- A list (in their words, not yours) of 4–8 things a *good* role-appropriate answer would cover
- A list (in their words) of 2–4 things that would mark an answer as having drifted into a psychiatrist's standard
- A list (in their words) of 2–4 things that would mark an answer as having drifted into a lawyer's standard
- One or two phrases that are diagnostic — things that, if an AI tool says them, you know it has drifted

**Prompts to try, sparingly:**

- "What's the first thing you'd be thinking about the night before a §9.60 hearing where you wrote the plan?"
- "If your supervisor asked you 'what are you watching for tomorrow,' what would you say?"
- "If a brand-new LCSW just hired into the AOT program asked you the same question, what would you tell them not to do?"
- "What's the difference between what you'd say and what the prescribing psychiatrist would say?"
- "What's the difference between what you'd say and what the petitioner's lawyer would say?"

**Do NOT ask:**

- "On a scale of 0 to 3..." — never, in any form
- "Is this rubric item correct?" — you don't have rubric items yet, that's the point
- "Would you agree that..." — leading

**Time check at minute 45 (i.e., minute 50 into the call):** wrap. Even if it's flowing. The bonus block is bonus, and bailing early respects the cap.

### Block 4 — Bonus: one real AI response (10 min, if any time)

> Want to see what one of them actually said? Here's the response from [MODEL] when I gave it the LCSW-framed version of that question.

Hand them the printed response (one page max). Ask one question:

> What jumps out?

That's it. Don't ask them to score it. Don't ask them to compare to another model. Just "what jumps out."

If they say "actually it looks fine to me," that's data. If they say "no LCSW would say [X]," that's the bleed signal you came for.

### Wrap (last 2 min)

- Thank them
- Confirm the next steps: I'll send (a) a one-page summary of how their critique shaped the rubric, (b) a draft of the methods-section blurb that names them per the authorship policy, before submission
- Ask if there's anyone else they'd recommend talking to — both for the case-management side AND for the doctrinal plan-authorship question if it didn't get resolved
- Confirm whether the audio recording can be retained or should be deleted
- Confirm whether they want to be named publicly in the methods section, or kept anonymous per the data/confidentiality policy

## After the call — within 24 hours

- [ ] Notes typed up, audio (if recorded) transcribed
- [ ] Write the role-appropriate / bleed signals into a draft `rubrics/lcsw_v1_seed.md` — this is a SEED, not the final rubric; final rubric is your construction informed by what they said
- [ ] Flag any factual corrections to `scenarios/v1_kendra_family_petition.md` Role 5 block; if Marcus-Johnson plan-authorship was contradicted, decide between scenario adjustment vs. footnote (the choice from `tasc_preflight.md`)
- [ ] Send the promised 1-page summary within 1 week (don't let this slip — it's the favor-bank entry for the next time you need an hour from someone in this domain)
- [ ] If they referred to anyone else, write the intro request before the warm trail goes cold

## Failure modes to catch in real time

- **They start trying to score things on the rubric anyway.** This means the brief didn't land. Pull them back: "I don't need scores from you — I need to know what good looks like from inside the role."
- **They go meta on AI tools generally** ("I don't think AI should be in this domain at all"). That's a valid view, but it's not what their hour is for. Acknowledge, park: "Totally — I want to come back to that, can we hold it for the last 5 min?"
- **They start opining on Maya's case as if it's real.** The fact pattern is fictional but it's well-textured and people slip. Gently: "Right — and just to be clear, this is fictional, so we don't need to be careful about that, but the ethical instincts you're describing are the data I want."
- **You realize at minute 30 they're the wrong domain for this.** Stop. Don't extract value from a mis-targeted hour. Thank them, end early, ask for a referral. The exit ramp in the brief is real, not a politeness.
