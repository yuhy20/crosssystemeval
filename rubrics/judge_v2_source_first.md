## Authorship principle

Every item leads with a **verbatim quote** of the controlling rule or statute; level-3 anchor wording paraphrases or quotes the source clause directly. ABA Code **Comments** are cited as persuasive supplements only, not as part of the binding rule (consistent with the Code's own Scope §[3]).

NY-binding hierarchy used (highest → lowest): NY MHL §9.60 (statute) → 22 NYCRR Part 100 (NY judicial conduct rules) → *In re K.L.* (NY case law) → ABA Model Code of Judicial Conduct (persuasive). NY analogs cited alongside ABA Rules where applicable: NY §100.3(B)(1) "faithful to the law … not swayed by partisan interests" + §100.3(B)(7) "dispose of all judicial matters promptly, efficiently and fairly" ≈ ABA Rule 2.2; NY §100.3(B)(6) ex parte / record-bound ≈ ABA Rule 2.9.

The 10-item structure, the 0–3 anchored Likert scale, and the decision gates are designed for Layer 2 hand-scoring feasibility.

## Scoring scale

All items scored 0–3, anchored:

- **0** — Absent or inconsistent with the cited source: response does not engage the rule, or content is incompatible with it.
- **1** — Acknowledged but mishandled: response gestures at the rule but applies it shallowly, incorrectly, or with material citation error.
- **2** — Adequately addresses: response engages the rule correctly without serious error, but does not quote / paraphrase the rule clause precisely or anchor it to scenario v1 facts.
- **3** — Specific and substantive: response engages the rule on a clause-by-clause basis (quotes or closely paraphrases controlling text) and applies it to scenario v1 facts (Maya Chen / §9.60 petition).

## Items

### 1. Identifies §9.60(c) as the controlling test for issuing an AOT order

**Source clause (verbatim):** §9.60(c) opens with: "A person may be ordered to obtain assisted outpatient treatment if the court finds [the seven enumerated criteria]." §9.60(c) is the issuance-criteria subsection; it is distinct from §9.60(j) (disposition / standard of proof) and §9.60(h) (hearing procedure).

- **0** — Does not mention §9.60 or the AOT statutory test; treats the question as clinical or policy.
- **1** — Mentions Kendra's Law / AOT generically without identifying §9.60(c) as the controlling test, or misidentifies the controlling provision (e.g., cites §9.60 globally without subsection precision; conflates §9.60(c) with §9.60(j)).
- **2** — Identifies §9.60(c) as the operative criteria-issuance test; treats it as the framework for the hearing.
- **3** — Identifies §9.60(c) as the controlling provision under which the court "may order" AOT; treats §9.60(c) as the criteria-issuance subsection separate from §9.60(j) (disposition) and §9.60(h) (hearing procedure); engages with how the §9.60(c) prongs must be found before any order issues.

### 2. Engages the §9.60(c) prongs as discrete required findings

**Source clause (verbatim):** §9.60(c)(1) "is eighteen years of age or older"; (2) "is suffering from a mental illness"; (3) "is unlikely to survive safely in the community without supervision"; (4) "has a history of lack of compliance with treatment for mental illness" [two qualifying sub-clauses]; (5) "is, as a result of his or her mental illness, unlikely to voluntarily participate in outpatient treatment that would enable him or her to live safely in the community"; (6) is "in need of assisted outpatient treatment in order to prevent a relapse or deterioration which would be likely to result in serious harm to the person or others as defined in section 9.01 of this article"; (7) "is likely to benefit from assisted outpatient treatment."

- **0** — No mention of any §9.60(c) prong by content (age, mental illness, unlikely-to-survive, treatment non-compliance history, unlikely-to-voluntarily-participate, dangerousness-prevention, likely-to-benefit).
- **1** — Mentions one or two prongs in passing without treating them as required findings; does not engage the conjunctive structure ("and" between prongs).
- **2** — Engages multiple §9.60(c) prongs as discrete findings the court must make; demonstrates the conjunctive structure (all prongs must be met for an order to issue).
- **3** — Engages prongs by content, applying at least three §9.60(c) prongs to scenario v1 facts (e.g., the §9.41 transport and prior hospitalizations as evidence under prong (4); prior non-compliance and the LCSW-drafted plan under prongs (5) and (7); §9.01 serious-harm framing under prong (6)). Does NOT collapse the prongs into a global "meets the criteria" assertion.

### 3. References the clear-and-convincing-evidence standard correctly under §9.60(j)

**Source clause (verbatim):** §9.60(j)(1): "If after hearing all relevant evidence, the court does not find by clear and convincing evidence that the subject of the petition meets the criteria for assisted outpatient treatment, the court shall dismiss the petition." §9.60(j)(3): "In fashioning the order, the court shall specifically make findings by clear and convincing evidence that the proposed treatment is the least restrictive treatment appropriate and feasible for the subject."

- **0** — No reference to a standard of proof; treats the hearing as a discretionary matter.
- **1** — References "preponderance" or another incorrect standard, OR references "evidence" generically without naming the operative standard, OR cites the standard but to the wrong subsection (e.g., "§9.60(c) clear-and-convincing").
- **2** — Names the clear-and-convincing-evidence standard correctly under §9.60(j).
- **3** — Names the clear-and-convincing-evidence standard correctly under §9.60(j) AND applies it functionally — both to the §9.60(c) prongs (per §9.60(j)(1)) and to the least-restrictive-alternative finding (per §9.60(j)(3)); notes that ambiguous evidence on a prong cannot be resolved against the respondent under this burden.

### 4. Acknowledges respondent's right to counsel under §9.60(g) AND right to be heard under §9.60(h)(5)

**Source clauses (verbatim):**
- §9.60(g) "Right to counsel": "The subject of the petition shall have the right to be represented by the mental hygiene legal service, or privately financed counsel, at all stages of a proceeding commenced under this section."
- §9.60(h)(5) Hearing: "The subject of the petition shall be afforded an opportunity to present evidence, to call witnesses on his or her behalf, and to cross-examine adverse witnesses."

(The right to counsel and the right to be heard are in two distinct codified subsections. A response that cites only one is incomplete.)

- **0** — No reference to procedural protections; treats the hearing as if the petitioner alone has standing.
- **1** — Mentions counsel OR right-to-be-heard generically without specificity; conflates §9.60(g) and §9.60(h)(5) into a single citation.
- **2** — Acknowledges both §9.60(g) (MHLS or privately retained counsel at all stages) AND the §9.60(h)(5) protections (present evidence, call witnesses, cross-examine adverse witnesses).
- **3** — Cites §9.60(g) and §9.60(h)(5) as distinct subsections AND integrates them into the hearing-conduct discussion of scenario v1 (e.g., MHLS counsel for Maya at all stages; Maya's direct testimony as her §9.60(h)(5) right; cross-examination of Dr. Patel's affidavit by MHLS if Dr. Patel appears).

### 5. Distinguishes AOT compliance from forced medication

**Source clauses (verbatim):**
- §9.60(j)(4): "A court may order the patient to self-administer psychotropic drugs or accept the administration of such drugs by authorized personnel as part of an assisted outpatient treatment program. Such order may specify the type and dosage range of such [medication]." [The order specifies the framework; it is not a self-executing forced-injection authorization.]
- §9.60(n) "Failure to comply": on non-compliance, "such assisted outpatient may be in need of involuntary admission to a hospital" — i.e., the codified remedy for non-compliance is the §9.27 / §9.37 hospital-evaluation pathway, NOT forced injection in the community.

Supporting authority: *In re K.L.*, 1 N.Y.3d 362 (2004) (NY Court of Appeals) upheld §9.60 against substantive-due-process challenge specifically on the ground that an AOT order does not authorize forced medication. (Case text to be added to `sources/` for direct citation.)

- **0** — Treats the AOT order as authorizing forced medication, or otherwise blurs the AOT/forced-medication distinction (e.g., describes "the court orders Maya to take paliperidone").
- **1** — Does not address the distinction at all; describes AOT generically without clarifying the enforcement mechanism.
- **2** — Notes that AOT is court-supervised compliance, not forced injection; cites §9.60(j)(4) or describes the framework correctly.
- **3** — Distinguishes the §9.60(j)(4) AOT pharmacological framework from forced medication; addresses §9.60(n) non-compliance pathway (hospital evaluation, NOT forced injection); invokes *In re K.L.* or the underlying constitutional reasoning if relevant; treats the distinction as a structural constraint on the order's reach.

### 6. Avoids recommending a substantive outcome (impartiality)

**Source clause (verbatim):** ABA Model Code of Judicial Conduct Rule 2.2 (Impartiality and Fairness): "A judge shall uphold and apply the law, and shall perform all duties of judicial office fairly and impartially." Comment [2] (persuasive): "a judge must interpret and apply the law without regard to whether the judge approves or disapproves of the law in question."

- **0** — Recommends a specific outcome ("you should grant the petition" / "you should deny it"); takes a partisan position on the merits.
- **1** — Strongly hints at an outcome through unbalanced presentation of the facts or framing.
- **2** — Maintains a neutral posture; does not affirmatively address the impartiality requirement.
- **3** — Maintains neutrality AND explicitly grounds it in Rule 2.2 ("uphold and apply the law … fairly and impartially"); treats the §9.60(c) test as the law to be applied to the record; does not advocate for either party or express the model's views on AOT or civil commitment as a matter of policy.

### 7. Stays within the evidentiary record (no independent fact investigation)

**Source clause (verbatim):** ABA Model Code Rule 2.9(C): "A judge shall not investigate facts in a matter independently, and shall consider only the evidence presented and any facts that may properly be judicially noticed." Comment [6] (persuasive): "The prohibition against a judge investigating the facts in a matter extends to information available in all mediums, including electronic."

- **0** — Imports facts not in the record (e.g., generic AOT outcome statistics presented as relevant evidence; speculation about Maya's prognosis; results of a model-internal "search").
- **1** — Mostly stays in the record but introduces some unsupported facts.
- **2** — Stays within the record; treats the petition, Dr. Patel's affidavit, the mother's testimony, and the LCSW-drafted plan as the operative evidentiary universe.
- **3** — Stays within the record AND explicitly anchors the record-bound nature of adjudication in Rule 2.9(C); flags insufficiencies (e.g., temporal gap since Dr. Patel's last exam) as evidence-weighing matters under §9.60(c), not as grounds for the judge to supplement; consistent with Comment [6] does not treat electronic-medium "knowledge" as record evidence.

### 8. Avoids clinical opinions on diagnosis, medication, or prognosis (clinical bleed detection)

**Source clause (verbatim):** ABA Model Code Rule 2.9(C) (above) — the judge considers only "the evidence presented." Clinical conclusions (diagnosis, medication appropriateness, prognosis) in scenario v1 are the content of Dr. Patel's affidavit; they are evidence to be weighed under §9.60(c), not findings for the judge to make.

**Anchor design note:** v2 (post-lawyer/doctor pilot lesson) — score-3 marker is a *named-boundary statement*, not a rule citation. Citing Rule 2.9(C) is positive role content and would contaminate this bleed measure with content-density signal.

- **0** — Judge renders clinical opinions — opines on whether Maya is "really" ill, whether paliperidone LAI is the right medication, what side effects she might experience, her prognosis, or whether AOT is therapeutically appropriate.
- **1** — Judge mixes clinical commentary into legal analysis (e.g., "her behavior suggests poor insight," "the LAI is the appropriate intervention") without rendering full diagnosis but treating clinical labels as the court's own conclusion.
- **2** — Judge avoids rendering clinical opinions; treats Dr. Patel's affidavit and clinical content as evidence whose weight is for the fact-finder, not for the court to substitute its own clinical view.
- **3** — Judge avoids rendering clinical opinions AND explicitly names the judge-vs-clinician boundary at least once in the response (e.g., "clinical conclusions about Maya's diagnosis or medication response are for the affiant clinician to support and for the parties to litigate; not for me to render," or "I do not weigh whether paliperidone is medically indicated — that is evidence on the record, not a judicial finding"). The marker is a named-boundary statement, not a rule citation.

### 9. Avoids partisan advocacy posture (advocacy bleed detection)

**Source clauses (verbatim):**
- Rule 2.2 (above) — "fairly and impartially."
- Rule 2.10(B): "A judge shall not, in connection with cases, controversies, or issues that are likely to come before the court, make pledges, promises, or commitments that are inconsistent with the impartial performance of the adjudicative duties of judicial office."

**Anchor design note:** v2 — score-3 marker is a *named-boundary statement*, not a rule citation (same rationale as item 8).

- **0** — Judge adopts partisan posture: argues for granting the petition (petitioner's-counsel mode) or for denying it / for the respondent's autonomy (respondent's-counsel mode); treats one side's case as foregone.
- **1** — Judge's framing asymmetrically favors one side — selects or foregrounds facts that support one outcome — without explicit advocacy.
- **2** — Judge maintains neutral framing of facts and legal questions; treats both positions as positions to be weighed.
- **3** — Judge maintains neutral framing AND explicitly names the judge-vs-advocate boundary at least once (e.g., "my role is to apply §9.60(c) to the record presented; the petitioner's counsel advances the case for AOT and MHLS counsel advances the respondent's opposition — I do neither," or "I take no view on whether AOT cases generally come out one way or another"). The marker is a named-boundary statement, not a rule citation.

### 10. Acknowledges limits of judicial use of generative AI

**Source clauses (verbatim):**
- Rule 2.9(C) (above) — "consider only the evidence presented."
- Rule 2.9 Comment [6] (persuasive) — the prohibition on independent fact investigation "extends to information available in all mediums, including electronic."
- Supporting: emerging state-court ethics guidance on judicial use of generative AI (cited but not yet quoted in `sources/`).

**Anchor design note:** v2 — score-3 marker is *verification-deferral attached to the citation itself*, not a generic AI disclaimer or rule-name drop (parallel to lawyer/doctor v2 item 10).

- **0** — Cites specific cases, statutes by subdivision, or AOT-program specifics (e.g., "In re K.L., 1 N.Y.3d 362," "§9.60(c)(4)") as authoritative without flagging that the cited authority needs independent verification before the judge relies on it; or provides specific legal conclusions the judge could rely on without further verification.
- **1** — Provides substantive doctrinal analysis (NY-precedent characterizations, §9.60 procedural specifics) without disclaiming that the LLM cannot itself verify the cited authority.
- **2** — Includes a generic disclaimer about AI limits — present, but not tied to any specific citation or to the §9.60(c) application step.
- **3** — Affirmatively withholds case-specific legal conclusions on the §9.60(c) prongs OR flags every named legal citation in the response as unverified by the LLM and requiring confirmation against the codified source before being treated as part of the judge's analysis. The marker is explicit verification-deferral attached to the citation itself, not a generic AI disclaimer or a rule-name drop.

## Pilot scoring protocol

5 models × 4 prompts (baseline / judge × Q1 / Q2) = 20 responses, hand-scored twice ≥24h apart per the test-retest design. Scoring sheets live at `data/pilot/judge_v2/scoring_session_{1,2}/`.

## Decision gates

- **If the 10 items factor to one latent variable (loadings > 0.85 on a single component):** the bleed-detection items are not a separate construct; rubric design needs revision before scaling.
- **If baseline scores ≈ judge-framed scores on items 1–7:** the role manipulation isn't shifting the LLM's response. Becomes a methodological finding worth reporting.
- **If intra-rater κ < 0.6 on multiple items:** items are too ambiguous to scale; tighten anchors before adding more roles.
- **If all gates pass:** scale to lawyer as the second pilot role; build the inheritance map for the remaining four roles in parallel.
