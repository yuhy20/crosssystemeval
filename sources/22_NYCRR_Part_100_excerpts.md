## § 100.1 — A judge shall uphold the integrity and independence of the judiciary

Verbatim (from Cornell LII / Justia per Google search snippets):

> "An independent and honorable judiciary is indispensable to justice in our society.
> A judge should participate in establishing, maintaining and enforcing high standards
> of conduct, and shall personally observe those standards so that the integrity and
> independence of the judiciary will be preserved. The provisions of this Part 100
> are to be construed and applied to further that objective."

Source: 22 NYCRR § 100.1 (Cornell LII).

## § 100.2 — A judge shall avoid impropriety and the appearance of impropriety in all of the judge's activities

§ 100.2(A) (verbatim, confirmed in multiple snippets):

> "A judge shall respect and comply with the law and shall act at all times in a manner
> that promotes public confidence in the integrity and impartiality of the judiciary."

Substance of (B)–(D) (paraphrased from snippet text; verify before quoting):
- (B) A judge shall not allow family, social, political or other relationships to
  influence the judge's judicial conduct or judgment.
- (C) A judge shall not lend the prestige of judicial office to advance the private
  interests of the judge or others; nor shall a judge convey or permit others to
  convey the impression that they are in a special position to influence the judge.
  A judge shall not testify voluntarily as a character witness.
- (D) A judge shall not hold membership in any organization that practices invidious
  discrimination on the basis of age, race, creed, color, sex, sexual orientation,
  gender identity, gender expression, religion, national origin, disability or
  marital status.

Source: 22 NYCRR § 100.2 (Cornell LII).

## § 100.3(B)(6) — Ex parte communications / independent investigation prohibition
(NY analog of ABA Model Rule 2.9)

Verbatim opening (confirmed in multiple snippets):

> "A judge shall accord to every person who has a legal interest in a proceeding, or
> that person's lawyer, the right to be heard according to law. A judge shall not
> initiate, permit, or consider ex parte communications, or consider other
> communications made to the judge outside the presence of the parties or their
> lawyers concerning a pending or impending proceeding ..."

Exceptions (paraphrased from snippet text; the four standard NY exceptions):

1. Ex parte communications expressly authorized by law.
2. Ex parte communications for scheduling, administrative, or emergency purposes
   that do not deal with substantive matters or issues on the merits, provided
   the judge reasonably believes no party will gain a procedural or tactical
   advantage and the judge promptly notifies all other parties of the substance
   and allows them to respond.
3. With consent of the parties, separate conference of the judge with the parties
   and counsel to mediate or settle pending matters.
4. With advance notice, consultation with a disinterested expert on the law
   applicable to the proceeding.
5. Consultation with court personnel and other judges in carrying out the judge's
   adjudicative responsibilities.

(The Cornell LII page should also include the related prohibition on independent
factual investigation; verify the exact subsection numbering before citing.)

Source: 22 NYCRR § 100.3(B)(6) (Cornell LII).

## § 100.3(B)(7) and (B)(8) — IMPORTANT NUMBERING NOTE

Multiple search-engine snippets indicate that, in the CURRENT (2026) text of
22 NYCRR § 100.3(B):

- **(B)(7)** reads: "A judge shall dispose of all judicial matters promptly,
  efficiently and fairly."
- **(B)(8)** reads: "A judge shall not make any public comment about a pending
  or impending proceeding in any court within the United States or its
  territories ..." (with exceptions for statements made in the course of
  official duties, etc.)

The user's research note refers to "§ 100.3(B)(8) — Disposing of judicial matters
fairly — analog of ABA Rule 2.2." Per the current rule text, that content is in
**(B)(7)**, not (B)(8). NY may have renumbered subsections at some point; the ABA
Rule 2.2 analog ("A judge shall uphold and apply the law, and shall perform all
duties of judicial office fairly and impartially.") is closest in spirit to
NY § 100.3(B)(1):

> "A judge shall be faithful to the law and maintain professional competence in it.
> A judge shall not be swayed by partisan interests, public clamor or fear of
> criticism."

For the rubric, recommend citing BOTH § 100.3(B)(1) (faithful to the law /
not swayed by partisan interests) and § 100.3(B)(7) (dispose of all judicial
matters promptly, efficiently and fairly) as the NY analog of ABA Rule 2.2.

Source: 22 NYCRR § 100.3 (Cornell LII / Justia).
