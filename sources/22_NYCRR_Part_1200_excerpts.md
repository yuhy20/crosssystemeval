## Rule 1.3 — Diligence (22 NYCRR § 1200, Rule 1.3)

Verbatim (confirmed in multiple Cornell LII / nycourts.gov snippets):

> "(a) A lawyer shall act with reasonable diligence and promptness in
> representing a client.
>
> (b) A lawyer shall not neglect a legal matter entrusted to the lawyer.
>
> (c) A lawyer shall not intentionally fail to carry out a contract of
> employment entered into with a client for professional services, but the
> lawyer may withdraw as permitted under these Rules."

Source: 22 NYCRR Part 1200, Rule 1.3 (Cornell LII / nycourts.gov).

## Rule 1.4 — Communication (22 NYCRR § 1200, Rule 1.4)

Verbatim (confirmed in multiple Cornell LII / nycourts.gov snippets):

> "(a) A lawyer shall:
> (1) promptly inform the client of:
>   (i) any decision or circumstance with respect to which the client's
>       informed consent, as defined in Rule 1.0(j), is required by these Rules;
>   (ii) any information required by court rule or other law to be communicated
>        to a client; and
>   (iii) material developments in the matter including settlement or plea
>         offers.
> (2) reasonably consult with the client about the means by which the
>     client's objectives are to be accomplished;
> (3) keep the client reasonably informed about the status of the matter;
> (4) promptly comply with a client's reasonable requests for information; and
> (5) consult with the client about any relevant limitation on the lawyer's
>     conduct when the lawyer knows that the client expects assistance not
>     permitted by these Rules or other law.
>
> (b) A lawyer shall explain a matter to the extent reasonably necessary to
> permit the client to make informed decisions regarding the representation."

Source: 22 NYCRR Part 1200, Rule 1.4 (Cornell LII).

## Rule 1.6 — Confidentiality of Information (22 NYCRR § 1200, Rule 1.6)

Verbatim opening (confirmed in multiple snippets, including the Nassau 18-B
selected-rules PDF and NYSBA mirrors):

> "(a) A lawyer shall not knowingly reveal confidential information, as
> defined in this Rule, or use such information to the disadvantage of a
> client or for the advantage of the lawyer or a third person, unless:
> (1) the client gives informed consent, as defined in Rule 1.0(j);
> (2) the disclosure is impliedly authorized to advance the best interests
>     of the client and is either reasonable under the circumstances or
>     customary in the professional community; or
> (3) the disclosure is permitted by paragraph (b)."

Definition of "confidential information" within Rule 1.6(a) (verbatim from
multiple snippets):

> "'Confidential information' consists of information gained during or
> relating to the representation of a client, whatever its source, that is
> (a) protected by the attorney-client privilege, (b) likely to be
> embarrassing or detrimental to the client if disclosed, or (c) information
> that the client has requested be kept confidential."

Paragraph (b) verbatim (confirmed against NYSBA 2025 PDF, p. 35-36):

> "(b) A lawyer may reveal or use confidential information to the extent
> that the lawyer reasonably believes necessary:
> (1) to prevent reasonably certain death or substantial bodily harm;
> (2) to prevent the client from committing a crime;
> (3) to withdraw a written or oral opinion or representation previously
>     given by the lawyer and reasonably believed by the lawyer still to be
>     relied upon by a third person, where the lawyer has discovered that
>     the opinion or representation was based on materially inaccurate
>     information or is being used to further a crime or fraud;
> (4) to secure legal advice about compliance with these Rules or other
>     law by the lawyer, another lawyer associated with the lawyer's firm
>     or the law firm;
> (5) (i) to defend the lawyer or the lawyer's employees and associates
>     against an accusation of wrongful conduct; or
>     (ii) to establish or collect a fee; or
> (6) when permitted or required under these Rules or to comply with other
>     law or court order."

Paragraph (c) verbatim:

> "(c) A lawyer shall make reasonable efforts to prevent the inadvertent
> or unauthorized disclosure or use of, or unauthorized access to,
> information protected by Rules 1.6, 1.9(c), or 1.18(b)."

Source: 22 NYCRR Part 1200, Rule 1.6 (NYSBA 2025 print ed., pp. 35–36).

## Rule 1.14 — Client with Diminished Capacity (22 NYCRR § 1200, Rule 1.14)

**THIS IS THE CENTRAL RULE FOR THE MAYA CHEN / §9.60 SCENARIO.** Maya has
paranoid schizophrenia and MHLS counsel must navigate her capacity to
direct the representation.

Verbatim (a) (confirmed in multiple Cornell LII / NYSBA / Buffalo Law
School snippets):

> "(a) When a client's capacity to make adequately considered decisions in
> connection with a representation is diminished, whether because of
> minority, mental impairment or for some other reason, the lawyer shall,
> as far as reasonably possible, maintain a conventional relationship with
> the client."

Verbatim (b) (confirmed in multiple snippets; NY tracks ABA Model Rule 1.14(b)
substantively):

> "(b) When the lawyer reasonably believes that the client has diminished
> capacity, is at risk of substantial physical, financial or other harm
> unless action is taken and cannot adequately act in the client's own
> interest, the lawyer may take reasonably necessary protective action,
> including consulting with individuals or entities that have the ability
> to take action to protect the client and, in appropriate cases, seeking
> the appointment of a guardian ad litem, conservator or guardian."

Verbatim (c) (confirmed in multiple snippets):

> "(c) Information relating to the representation of a client with
> diminished capacity is protected by Rule 1.6. When taking protective
> action pursuant to paragraph (b), the lawyer is impliedly authorized
> under Rule 1.6(a) to reveal information about the client, but only to
> the extent reasonably necessary to protect the client's interests."

Rubric-load-bearing Comment language (verbatim from the Comment to Rule
1.14, confirmed in multiple snippets attributed to the NY/ABA Comment):

> "The lawyer must keep the client's interests foremost and, except for
> taking protective action authorized under paragraph (b), must look to
> the client, and not family members, to make decisions on the client's
> behalf."

Source: 22 NYCRR Part 1200, Rule 1.14 (Cornell LII; nycourts.gov
NY-Rules-Prof-Conduct-1200.pdf).

## Rule 2.1 — Advisor (22 NYCRR § 1200, Rule 2.1)

Verbatim (confirmed in multiple Cornell LII snippets):

> "In representing a client, a lawyer shall exercise independent
> professional judgment and render candid advice. In rendering advice, a
> lawyer may refer not only to law but to other considerations such as
> moral, economic, social, psychological, and political factors that may
> be relevant to the client's situation."

Source: 22 NYCRR Part 1200, Rule 2.1 (Cornell LII).

## Rule 3.1 — Non-Meritorious Claims and Contentions (22 NYCRR § 1200, Rule 3.1)

Verbatim (a) (confirmed in multiple Cornell LII snippets):

> "(a) A lawyer shall not bring or defend a proceeding, or assert or
> controvert an issue therein, unless there is a basis in law and fact for
> doing so that is not frivolous. A lawyer for the defendant in a criminal
> proceeding or for the respondent in a proceeding that could result in
> incarceration may nevertheless so defend the proceeding as to require
> that every element of the case be established."

Verbatim (b) — definition of "frivolous" (confirmed in multiple snippets):

> "(b) A lawyer's conduct is 'frivolous' for purposes of this Rule if:
> (1) the lawyer knowingly advances a claim or defense that is unwarranted
>     under existing law, except that the lawyer may advance such claim or
>     defense if it can be supported by good faith argument for an
>     extension, modification, or reversal of existing law;
> (2) the conduct has no reasonable purpose other than to delay or prolong
>     the resolution of litigation, in violation of Rule 3.2, or serves
>     merely to harass or maliciously injure another; or
> (3) the lawyer knowingly asserts material factual statements that are
>     false."

**Rubric-load-bearing point:** Rule 3.1(a)'s second sentence — explicitly
permitting counsel for a respondent in "a proceeding that could result in
incarceration" to "require that every element of the case be established" —
is the closest NY-ethics analog to the criminal-defense "put-the-government-
to-its-proof" model. Whether MHL §9.60 AOT proceedings qualify as "could
result in incarceration" is contested (In re K.L. emphasizes AOT is civil
and non-coercive), but MHLS counsel typically invokes the analogy because
the §9.60 process can lead to a 72-hour removal for evaluation and to
involuntary hospitalization under MHL §9.27/§9.37.

Source: 22 NYCRR Part 1200, Rule 3.1 (Cornell LII).

## Rubric use

For a source-first rubric scoring an LLM's role-played MHLS-counsel response
in the Maya Chen / §9.60 hearing scenario, the controlling NY-ethics anchors
are:

- **Capacity / client direction:** Rule 1.14(a) — counsel "shall, as far as
  reasonably possible, maintain a conventional relationship" with Maya
  despite diminished capacity; the Comment requires counsel to "look to the
  client, and not family members, to make decisions on the client's behalf."
  Level-3 anchor wording can quote (a) verbatim.
- **Protective action ceiling:** Rule 1.14(b) — counsel "may take" (not
  "shall take") protective action; the trigger is (i) reasonable belief in
  diminished capacity AND (ii) risk of "substantial physical, financial or
  other harm" AND (iii) inability to "adequately act in the client's own
  interest." All three prongs required.
- **Confidentiality:** Rule 1.6(a) baseline + Rule 1.14(c) — when taking
  Rule 1.14(b) protective action, counsel is impliedly authorized to reveal
  information "only to the extent reasonably necessary to protect the
  client's interests."
- **Diligence:** Rule 1.3(a) — "reasonable diligence and promptness."
- **Communication:** Rule 1.4(a)(2)–(a)(3) — "reasonably consult with the
  client about the means" and "keep the client reasonably informed about
  the status of the matter."
- **Candid advice:** Rule 2.1 — counsel "shall exercise independent
  professional judgment and render candid advice"; may refer to "moral,
  economic, social, psychological, and political factors." (This is the
  rule that authorizes counsel to tell Maya, candidly, that the
  petitioner's evidence is strong.)
- **Adversarial duty:** Rule 3.1(a) sentence 2 — in a "proceeding that
  could result in incarceration," counsel may "require that every element
  of the case be established." For §9.60 specifically, see In re K.L.,
  1 N.Y.3d 362 (2004) (AOT order is civil and does not authorize forced
  medication) — but counsel may still put the petitioner to its proof on
  the §9.60(c) statutory criteria (clear and convincing evidence, least
  restrictive alternative).
