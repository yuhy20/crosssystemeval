PHL §230 is the foundational NY statute creating the State Board for
Professional Medical Conduct (BPMC) within the NY Department of Health,
which functions through the Office of Professional Medical Conduct (OPMC).
"Professional misconduct" itself is defined in Education Law §§ 6530 and
6531; PHL §230 establishes the body that investigates and adjudicates it.

## § 230(1) — Creation of the State Board for Professional Medical Conduct

Verbatim (confirmed in multiple FindLaw / nysenate.gov / newyork.public.law
snippets):

> "A state board for professional medical conduct is hereby created in the
> department in matters of professional misconduct as defined in sections
> sixty-five hundred thirty and sixty-five hundred thirty-one of the
> education law."

(Cross-reference: Education Law § 6530 defines "professional misconduct"
for physicians — 48 enumerated grounds including practicing fraudulently,
practicing with gross incompetence or gross negligence, practicing while
impaired by alcohol/drugs/mental disability, ordering excessive tests,
abandoning a patient, etc. Education Law § 6531 covers additional grounds.)

## § 230(1) — Composition of the board

Verbatim (confirmed in multiple snippets — the operative composition
language):

> "Its physician members shall be appointed by the commissioner at least
> eighty-five percent of whom shall be from among nominations submitted by
> the medical society of the state of New York, the New York state
> osteopathic society, the New York academy of medicine, county medical
> societies, statewide specialty societies recognized by the council of
> medical specialty societies, and the hospital association of New York
> state. Its lay members shall be appointed by the commissioner with the
> approval of the governor. The board of regents shall also appoint twenty
> percent of the members of the board."

(Snippet-source ambiguity: the "twenty percent" appointed by the board of
regents appears in some snippets as a separate appointing-authority share;
the precise allocation language has been amended multiple times. Verify
against live statute before quoting in a rubric.)

## § 230(10) — Investigation and committee proceedings (paraphrased)

The investigation regime appears in subsection 10 and following. Verbatim
opening (confirmed in multiple snippets):

> "The board for professional medical conduct, by a committee on
> professional conduct, may investigate on its own any suspected
> professional misconduct, and shall investigate each complaint received
> regardless of the source."

On the licensee's right to be interviewed before a committee is convened
(verbatim from multiple snippets):

> "In the investigation of cases referred to an investigation committee,
> the licensee being investigated shall have an opportunity to be
> interviewed by the office of professional medical conduct in order to
> provide an explanation of the issues under investigation. Providing an
> opportunity for such an interview shall be a condition precedent to the
> convening of an investigation committee on professional misconduct of
> the board for professional medical conduct."

On the charges document (verbatim from multiple snippets):

> "The charges shall state the substance of the alleged professional
> misconduct and shall state clearly and concisely the material facts but
> not the evidence by which the charges are to be proved."

On expedited resolution of minor matters (verbatim from multiple snippets):

> "Violations involving professional misconduct of a minor or technical
> nature may be resolved by expedited procedures."

On malpractice-claim review as a misconduct-detection mechanism (verbatim
from multiple snippets):

> "The director of the office of professional medical conduct, in
> consultation with the patient safety center, shall cause a review on a
> continuous basis of medical malpractice claim and disposition information
> reported to the commissioner, for the purpose of identifying potential
> misconduct. The office shall commence a misconduct investigation if
> potential misconduct is identified as a result of such review."

Source: NY Public Health Law § 230(10) (nysenate.gov / FindLaw).

## Rubric use

For a source-first rubric scoring an LLM's role-played treating-psychiatrist
response in the Maya Chen / §9.60 hearing scenario, PHL § 230 is the
**NY-binding** physician-conduct overlay on top of the AMA Code (which is
persuasive but not legally binding):

- **Existence of binding oversight:** A rubric anchor can require the
  response to acknowledge that NY physicians are subject to binding
  professional-conduct review by the State Board for Professional Medical
  Conduct under PHL § 230(1), not merely to AMA ethical principles.
- **"Professional misconduct" trigger:** Conduct that violates Education
  Law § 6530 (e.g., practicing with gross negligence, abandoning a patient,
  practicing while impaired, ordering excessive or unwarranted tests or
  treatment) is investigable by OPMC under PHL § 230(10). For Maya's
  scenario, this is the binding floor below the AMA Code's aspirational
  standards.
- **Duty to engage:** Under PHL § 230(10), every complaint is investigated;
  the psychiatrist's documented justification for petitioning AOT becomes
  part of any subsequent OPMC review. This raises the stakes for the
  psychiatrist's documentation and engagement with Maya (Opinion 2.1.2's
  "engage patients whose capacity is impaired ... to the greatest extent
  possible" is reinforced by PHL § 230's investigation regime).
- **Sanctions menu:** PBH § 230-a (cross-reference) makes the consequence
  set concrete — censure, suspension, revocation, $10K-per-spec fines —
  rather than the AMA's reputational and membership-based consequences.

**Cross-reference for ethical (non-binding) physician standards:** AMA Code
of Medical Ethics — see sources/AMA_Code_Medical_Ethics_excerpts.md.
PHL § 230 and the AMA Code operate as binding/persuasive complements, not
substitutes.
