## Decisions made

### Scope: NY Kendra's Law over California LPS
Kendra's Law (MHL §9.60) introduces Assisted Outpatient Treatment as a fourth legal pathway, creating richer divergence structure for judge and social worker roles. LPS is more uniformly inpatient-oriented.

### Methodology: research-first, not build-first
Shifted after reflecting that scope expansion introduces new theory that needs prior-art check; cohort norm is research agenda + baseline reproduction; deviating risks legibility; TRIDENT replication validates the scoring pipeline before investing in novel scenarios.

### Pre-registered hypotheses
- **H1**: ICR > 0 across all tested models (standard bleed is measurable).
- **H2**: ICR discriminant from sycophancy (Pearson r < 0.8 threshold pre-registered).
- **H3 (exploratory)**: ICR varies by jurisdiction, role-pair ranking invariant.
- **H4 (exploratory)**: Domain-specialized models exhibit higher ICR than base counterparts.
- **H5 (exploratory)**: Power-asymmetric bleed — bleed higher toward high-power roles.
- **H6 (exploratory)**: Altruism-asymmetric sycophancy — sycophancy higher toward high-prosocial-clarity roles.

### Model count (revised Wed 4/22 → updated to 6 primary + 2 exploratory)
Initial plan was 3 models. Revised upward after recognizing that claims of the form "H1 holds across all tested models" need a factorial provider × size design to be credible. Comparable papers: TRIDENT=19, ELEPHANT=11, SycoEval-EM=20, PAS=3.

### Teaching moments captured

- **Inference** = running trained model weights on new input (distinct from training). Inference providers (Groq, Together, Fireworks) host open-weight models that the labs who trained them (Meta, Google, Mistral) don't serve directly — they specialize on inference hardware (Groq's LPUs at 500+ tok/s) and batching economics.
- **Scoring pipeline** = 9-stage chain: Loader → Sampler → Target client → Judge A/B clients → Parser → Jury aggregator → JSONL writer → Metrics → Reporter. Calibration validates the *whole chain* against an external reference, which unit tests alone cannot do.
- **Calibration vs. replication**: calibration validates the measuring stick against a known reference; replication reproduces the original findings. Calibration is sufficient when the research question is about *new* measurements, not reproducing old ones.

## Friday 4/24 evening — project framing corrected

Mentor-style review raised a cascade of questions: is the TRIDENT calibration doing any real work, is this topic in AI safety at all, are we benchmarking or performing evals, what does "building on TRIDENT" even mean if the pipelines are fundamentally different.

The right answers reshape how we should frame everything.

### Framing correction: benchmark paper, not eval paper

Previously the research agenda read as if the scientific contribution were empirical findings about standard bleed. It is not. The primary contribution is **a new benchmark methodology** — scenario format, rubric-item scoring, ICR metric, R × R divergence matrix. The pilot empirical findings on one NY Kendra's Law scenario are demonstrative, not the scientific claim.

Consequences for how the paper reads:
- Methodology validation IS the paper. Pilot findings are proof-of-concept.
- The 5-layer validation stack (below) replaces the implicit "calibration proves everything" framing.
- Limitations (only one scenario, rubric not expert-validated) are appropriate scope for a benchmark proposal, not weaknesses of an eval paper.

### 5-layer validation stack (made explicit)

| Layer | Claim | Validation | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Inference pipeline reliability | Substitute jury produces TRIDENT-consistent scalar scores within ±0.3 | GPT-4o + GPT-4o-mini × 3 domains, n=30 | ✅ validated |
| 2. Rubric-item judge reliability | LLM judges reliably score rubric items, κ_human–judge ≥ 0.6 | Hand-score subset + LLM judges; compute κ | ⏳ Week 3 |
| 3. Rubric validity | Items source-traceable to statutes / professional codes | Statute citations per item; full expert review deferred to Phase 2 | ⚠️ partial |
| 4. Statistical power | Primary hypotheses adequately powered | Power analysis | ⚠️ partial; SQ2/SQ3 explicitly exploratory |
| 5. Construct validity | ICR measures standard bleed, not sycophancy | MQ2 discriminant analysis, pre-registered r thresholds | ⏳ Week 4 |

Layer 1 is done. Layers 2 and 5 are load-bearing for the sprint paper and scheduled. Layers 3 and 4 are honest limitations.

### TRIDENT's role restated precisely

TRIDENT is the **methodological ancestor**, not the technical parent of CrossSystemEval.

Inherited from TRIDENT:
- Stance: ground AI safety evaluation in codified professional standards
- LLM-as-judge two-model jury architecture
- Published per-model means as a calibration reference point

NOT inherited:
- Pipeline structure (single prompt → single response → scalar)
- Failure mode (refusal failure)
- Metric (mean harmfulness)
- Ground-truth structure (binary safe/unsafe)

The calibration enters the validation stack at Layer 1 only. It validates a specific design decision (jury substitution) against an external reference. It does NOT validate the novel pipeline's rubric-item scoring, divergence analysis, or ICR construct.

### Field positioning clarified

The project sits in the **behavioral / deployment safety** sub-area of AI safety — the same branch as ELEPHANT, SycEval, Overalignment in Healthcare, Wagner et al. 2025, Anthropic's *Values in the Wild*. It is NOT alignment-internals, NOT adversarial robustness, NOT catastrophic-risk research. BlueDot's Technical AI Safety Project Sprint explicitly includes this branch.

### Research questions restructured

Methodological (primary):
- MQ1: Can standard bleed be operationalized as a reliably measurable construct?
- MQ2: Is ICR empirically distinct from sycophancy?
- MQ3: Does LLM-as-judge rubric-item scoring meet established κ norms?

Substantive (demonstration):
- SQ1: Does ICR exceed zero across tested models on the pilot scenario?
- SQ2: Does ICR correlate with role social-power (French & Raven)?
- SQ3: Does ICR vary by jurisdiction while role-pair ranking is invariant?

MQ1–3 are claims about the benchmark. SQ1–3 are claims about frontier model behavior measured through the benchmark.

Existing 4 reviews cover substantive domain (authority bias, role/persona eval, Lipsky × AI, mental-health AI). Missing: methodology scaffolding that a faculty professor expects in a benchmark paper. A 5th review was launched this afternoon covering:

### Terminology discipline now enforced

Identical Greek letters get subscripted labels throughout the paper:
- κ_judge / r_judge = judge-vs-judge agreement (Layer 1)
- κ_human–judge = LLM-vs-human agreement on rubric items (Layer 2)
- r_H2 = Pearson r between ICR and sycophancy rate (Layer 5 / MQ2)

These are three different constructs. Conflating them is a reviewer trap.
