## Decisions made (with reasoning trail)

### Expert outreach over single-rater analysis
Documented above. The pilot's intra-rater κ tells you whether the *author* is consistent with the *author*. Layer 2 requires κ_human–judge ≥ 0.6 against someone who is not the author. Without a lined-up expert, Layer 2 can't happen, and the pilot's value is capped at "design check" rather than "validation result." Operationalizing the two informal offers was therefore the highest-leverage thing in the week, even at the cost of the analysis itself slipping. The risk is that *neither* helper engages, in which case the week paid for nothing — flagged in the risks below.

### Six responses, not twelve, for the lawyer
The lawyer's hour is the binding constraint. 12 responses × 10 items × ~3 minutes per item = ~6 hours, which is unrealistic and would either produce rushed scoring or non-completion. Cut to 6 responses (3 models × 2 framings, Q1 held constant) gives 6 × 10 × 3 min = ~3 hours, which is still over the hour cap. Brief is therefore framed as "60 min hard cap; if at minute 20 the rubric is wrong, stop scoring and tell me that — that's the most valuable signal." Q1 was chosen as the held-constant question because the baseline-q1 cell is where the strongest standard-bleed signal was observed in Week 2 (Claude inferred petitioner's-counsel and adopted the role unprompted).

### Single authorship-policy template applied to both briefs
Three options bracketed: acknowledgement-only / coauthorship / paid consultation. Same policy applies to both helpers unless there's a strong reason to differ. The template is in `outreach/authorship_template.md` and is referenced as `[INSERT FILLED-IN AUTHORSHIP POLICY]` in both briefs — settle once, paste twice. Doing this upfront (rather than after the work is done) protects both the helper from ambiguity and the project from later credit disputes.

### TASC engagement is a three-step protocol, not a one-shot
TASC's standard work is criminal-court diversion (drugs, felony alternatives-to-incarceration), not civil-court §9.60 AOT. The jurisdictional fit is plausible but not certain. Rather than send the brief and the call invite together, the protocol is: pre-flight first (10 min async, two yes/no questions + one fact-check), and the brief + call only go out if pre-flight clears. The pre-flight question doubles as the cheapest possible path to resolve the Marcus-Johnson plan-authorship question (open question 3 from Week 2) — a TASC case-manager may know that off the top of their head.
