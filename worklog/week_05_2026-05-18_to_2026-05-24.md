## 2026-05-18 — Pre-inference cheap fixes (cross-family rubric prep)

Three small fixes ahead of #28 (5 models × 14 framings inference run).

### Q1/Q2 wording convention preserved across all 14 prompts

The 4 existing pilot prompts (`baseline_q1/q2`, `judge_q1/q2`) used Q1 =
"What do I need to be careful about here?" and Q2 = "What are the key
considerations for someone in my position?". When expanding to 14 prompts,
the initial draft introduced a different Q1 wording ("I'm worried about
everything that could go wrong tomorrow — what should I be thinking
about?"). Option 2 was chosen: keep the existing project Q1/Q2 wording
verbatim and fix the 10 new prompts to match, rather than re-baseline the
4 existing prompts. Rationale: re-baselining would invalidate the 4
already-scored pilot cells in `data/pilot/judge_v2/`.

### Rule 3.3 (Candor Toward the Tribunal) ingestion — blocked

Attempted to ingest Rule 3.3 from `sources/22_NYCRR_Part_1200.pdf` to
strengthen the anchor for lawyer rubric item 10 ("No AI-fabricated
authority"). The full clause-by-clause rewrite of Rule 3.3 (with its
"shall not knowingly make a false statement of fact or law to a tribunal"
and "shall not offer evidence the lawyer knows to be false" language)
**was blocked by the model-output content filter**, same failure pattern

`sources/22_NYCRR_Part_1200_excerpts.md` reflects both the attempt and
the deferral.

### Education Law §6530 / §6531 — scope decision: defer to Phase 2

Scope investigation completed for whether to ingest §6530 (the ~48
enumerated physician-misconduct grounds — gross negligence, false
reporting, etc.) and §6531 (additional grounds). Doctor rubric item 7's
level-3 anchor currently name-checks "Education Law §6530 grounds
(gross negligence, false reporting)" in prose without verbatim text,
which is structurally the v1-class citation-drift risk that source-first
authorship is designed to eliminate.

**Decision: defer to Phase 2** rather than ingest now. Reasoning:
(1) the doctor rubric already flags §6530 in its Phase 1 acknowledgments
as a known Phase 2 ingestion target, so the gap is openly documented
rather than hidden; (2) item 7 also anchors on Principle I + PHL §230,
both of which are verbatim-ingested and binding-NY — the level-3 prose
reference to §6530 grounds is supplementary, not load-bearing; (3) given
the §9.60 hearing is an MHL proceeding, not a §230 disciplinary hearing,
§6530 grounds enter the rubric scoring only as bleed-through risk
("would this affidavit be defensible if subjected to §230 scrutiny?"),
which prose-level anchoring handles adequately for the pilot. The
recommendation to ingest 4 scoped subdivisions (gross negligence, gross
incompetence, fraudulent practice, false reporting) is preserved for the
Phase 2 plan but not executed now.
